# Equus zebra hartmannae

**Common Name:** Hartmann's Mountain Zebra

**Scientific Name:** *Equus zebra hartmannae*

## Conservation Status

Hartmann's mountain zebra is a subspecies of the mountain zebra (*Equus zebra*), which is classified as **Vulnerable (VU)** according to the International Union for Conservation of Nature (IUCN) Red List. Although populations have recovered in some protected areas, they remain threatened by habitat fragmentation, drought, and competition with livestock.

## Habitat

Hartmann's mountain zebras inhabit rugged mountainous regions, rocky hillsides, escarpments, and arid grasslands of Namibia and southwestern Angola. They are well adapted to steep, uneven terrain where they can evade predators and find sparse vegetation.

## Behaviour

Hartmann's mountain zebras are highly social and typically live in small family groups consisting of one stallion, several mares, and their foals. Young males often form bachelor groups before establishing their own harems. They are active during the cooler parts of the day and can travel long distances in search of food and water.

## Diet

Hartmann's mountain zebras are herbivorous grazers that feed primarily on grasses. During dry seasons, they also consume shrubs, leaves, bark, and herbs when grasses become scarce. They are capable of surviving in dry environments by travelling considerable distances to reach water sources.

## Ecological Importance

As large grazing herbivores, Hartmann's mountain zebras help maintain healthy grassland ecosystems by controlling vegetation growth and dispersing plant seeds. They also serve as prey for large predators such as lions, leopards, and spotted hyenas.

## Major Threats

- Habitat fragmentation
- Competition with livestock for grazing resources
- Prolonged droughts
- Human disturbance and fencing that restrict movement

## Interesting Fact

Hartmann's mountain zebra is an excellent climber and is specially adapted to steep, rocky terrain. Its hard, pointed hooves provide exceptional grip, allowing it to move confidently across mountain slopes where many other large herbivores cannot.
