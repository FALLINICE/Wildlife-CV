# Giraffa camelopardalis

**Common Name:** Giraffe

**Scientific Name:** *Giraffa camelopardalis*

## Conservation Status

**Vulnerable (VU)** according to the International Union for Conservation of Nature (IUCN) Red List. Giraffe populations have declined over recent decades due to habitat loss, human population growth, illegal hunting, and civil unrest in parts of their range.

## Habitat

Giraffes inhabit savannas, open woodlands, and dry grasslands throughout sub-Saharan Africa. They are commonly found in areas with abundant acacia trees and other woody vegetation that provide their primary food source.

## Behaviour

Giraffes are social animals that live in loose, fluid groups called towers. Group membership changes frequently, and individuals associate freely without maintaining permanent herds. They are generally peaceful but males engage in combat, known as "necking," by swinging their long necks and heads to establish dominance and breeding rights.

## Diet

Giraffes are herbivorous browsers that feed primarily on leaves, buds, flowers, and seed pods from trees and shrubs, especially acacia species. Their long necks and prehensile tongues, which can reach up to 45 centimetres (18 inches), allow them to access foliage beyond the reach of most other herbivores.

## Ecological Importance

As important browsers, giraffes help shape vegetation structure and promote healthy tree growth. They also disperse seeds through their digestive system, contributing to plant regeneration and maintaining biodiversity within savanna ecosystems.

## Major Threats

- Habitat loss and fragmentation
- Illegal hunting and poaching
- Human-wildlife conflict
- Disease and civil unrest in parts of their range

## Interesting Fact

The giraffe is the world's tallest living land animal. Adult males can reach heights of over 5.5 metres (18 feet). Despite their long necks, giraffes have the same number of neck vertebrae—seven—as most other mammals, including humans.
