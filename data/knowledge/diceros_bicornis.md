# Diceros bicornis

**Common Name:** Black Rhinoceros (Black Rhino)

**Scientific Name:** *Diceros bicornis*

## Conservation Status

**Critically Endangered (CR)** according to the International Union for Conservation of Nature (IUCN) Red List. Although conservation efforts have helped populations recover from historic declines, black rhinos remain at high risk of extinction due to ongoing poaching and habitat loss.

## Habitat

Black rhinos inhabit savannas, shrublands, dry woodlands, and semi-desert regions across eastern and southern Africa. They prefer areas with dense shrubs and bushes that provide both food and cover.

## Behaviour

Black rhinos are generally solitary and territorial animals. Adult females may be accompanied by their calves, while adult males maintain territories that overlap with those of several females. Despite their poor eyesight, black rhinos have an excellent sense of smell and acute hearing, allowing them to detect threats and locate food.

## Diet

Black rhinos are herbivorous browsers that feed primarily on leaves, twigs, shoots, shrubs, and small trees. Their distinctive hooked upper lip is specially adapted for grasping and stripping vegetation from branches.

## Ecological Importance

As large browsers, black rhinos help shape vegetation structure by controlling the growth of woody plants. This promotes plant diversity and helps maintain healthy savanna and woodland ecosystems that support many other species.

## Major Threats

- Illegal poaching for rhinoceros horns
- Habitat loss and fragmentation
- Human-wildlife conflict
- Small, isolated populations that reduce genetic diversity

## Interesting Fact

Unlike the white rhinoceros, which mainly grazes on grasses, the black rhino is a browser. Its pointed, prehensile upper lip is perfectly adapted for feeding on shrubs and tree branches rather than grass.
