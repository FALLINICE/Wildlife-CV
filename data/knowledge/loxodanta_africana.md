# Loxodonta africana

**Common Name:** African Bush Elephant

**Scientific Name:** *Loxodonta africana*

## Conservation Status

**Endangered (EN)** according to the International Union for Conservation of Nature (IUCN) Red List. African bush elephant populations have declined significantly over recent decades due to poaching for ivory, habitat loss, and increasing conflict with humans.

## Habitat

African bush elephants inhabit a wide range of environments, including savannas, grasslands, woodlands, forests, and wetlands across sub-Saharan Africa. They require access to large areas with sufficient food and water.

## Behaviour

African bush elephants are highly intelligent and social animals that live in family groups led by an experienced female known as the matriarch. These herds consist mainly of related females and their young, while adult males typically live alone or form small bachelor groups. Elephants communicate using vocalizations, body language, touch, and low-frequency sounds (infrasound) that can travel several kilometres.

## Diet

African bush elephants are herbivores and spend much of the day feeding. Their diet includes grasses, leaves, bark, roots, fruits, and branches. An adult elephant can consume over 100 kilograms (220 pounds) of vegetation and drink more than 100 litres (26 gallons) of water in a single day.

## Ecological Importance

African bush elephants are considered a **keystone species** because they play a vital role in maintaining healthy ecosystems. By knocking down trees, dispersing seeds through their dung, and creating water access points used by other animals, they help shape and sustain African landscapes.

## Major Threats

- Illegal poaching for ivory
- Habitat loss and fragmentation
- Human-elephant conflict due to agricultural expansion
- Climate change affecting water and food availability

## Interesting Fact

The African bush elephant is the largest living land animal. Adult males can stand up to 4 metres (13 feet) tall at the shoulder and weigh over 6,000 kilograms (13,000 pounds). Their trunks contain around 40,000 muscles, allowing them to perform both powerful and highly delicate tasks.
