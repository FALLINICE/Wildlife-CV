# Canis mesomelas

**Common Name:** Black-backed Jackal

**Scientific Name:** *Canis mesomelas*

## Conservation Status

**Least Concern (LC)** according to the International Union for Conservation of Nature (IUCN) Red List. The black-backed jackal has a wide distribution across eastern and southern Africa and maintains stable populations due to its adaptability to a variety of habitats.

## Habitat

Black-backed jackals inhabit savannas, grasslands, open woodlands, scrublands, semi-desert regions, and coastal areas. They are highly adaptable and can thrive in both protected reserves and landscapes modified by human activity.

## Behaviour

Black-backed jackals are intelligent, highly territorial, and typically live in monogamous pairs that maintain territories year-round. They are most active during the early morning, evening, and night. Family members cooperate in defending their territory and raising pups, with offspring from previous litters often helping to care for younger siblings.

## Diet

Black-backed jackals are opportunistic omnivores. Their diet includes small mammals, birds, reptiles, insects, fruits, carrion, and occasionally livestock. They frequently scavenge alongside larger predators such as lions and hyenas but are also skilled hunters capable of catching their own prey.

## Ecological Importance

As both predators and scavengers, black-backed jackals play an important role in maintaining ecosystem health. They help regulate populations of small animals, remove carcasses that could spread disease, and contribute to nutrient recycling within their habitats.

## Major Threats

- Persecution by humans due to livestock predation
- Habitat degradation in some regions
- Disease transmission from domestic dogs
- Road mortality

## Interesting Fact

The black-backed jackal is named for the distinctive black-and-silver saddle of fur running along its back. It is known for forming lifelong pair bonds, and mated pairs often remain together for many years while jointly defending their territory and raising offspring.
