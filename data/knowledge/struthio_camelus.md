# Struthio camelus

**Common Name:** Common Ostrich

**Scientific Name:** *Struthio camelus*

## Conservation Status

**Least Concern (LC)** according to the International Union for Conservation of Nature (IUCN) Red List. The common ostrich has a wide distribution across Africa and maintains stable populations in many protected areas, although some local populations have declined due to habitat loss, hunting, and egg collection.

## Habitat

Common ostriches inhabit open landscapes including savannas, grasslands, semi-arid plains, and open woodlands. They are found throughout much of eastern and southern Africa, where open terrain allows them to detect predators from long distances.

## Behaviour

The common ostrich is the world's largest living bird and is flightless. Instead of flying, it relies on its powerful legs to escape predators, reaching speeds of up to 70 km/h (43 mph). Ostriches are typically active during the day and often live in small groups or mixed herds with grazing mammals such as zebras and antelopes.

## Diet

Common ostriches are omnivorous but feed mainly on grasses, seeds, leaves, flowers, fruits, and other plant material. They also consume insects, small reptiles, and other invertebrates when available. Like many birds, they swallow small stones (gastroliths) to help grind food in their gizzard.

## Ecological Importance

As large herbivores and seed dispersers, ostriches contribute to the maintenance of savanna ecosystems. Their feeding habits help disperse plant seeds, while their eggs and chicks provide food for predators such as jackals and large birds of prey.

## Major Threats

- Habitat degradation and fragmentation
- Hunting for meat, feathers, skin, and eggs
- Human disturbance in some regions
- Predation on eggs and chicks

## Interesting Fact

The common ostrich lays the largest eggs of any living bird. Although their eggs are the largest by size, they are relatively small compared to the adult bird's body size. Ostriches also have the largest eyes of any land animal, measuring about 5 cm (2 inches) in diameter.
