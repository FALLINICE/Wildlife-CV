# Oryx gazella

**Common Name:** Gemsbok

**Scientific Name:** *Oryx gazella*

## Conservation Status

**Least Concern (LC)** according to the International Union for Conservation of Nature (IUCN) Red List. Although populations are generally stable across much of their range, local declines can occur due to habitat degradation, fencing, and overhunting.

## Habitat

The gemsbok inhabits arid and semi-arid environments, including deserts, dry grasslands, shrublands, and open savannas. It is widely distributed across southern Africa, particularly in Namibia, Botswana, South Africa, and parts of western Zimbabwe.

## Behaviour

Gemsboks are highly adapted to hot, dry climates and can survive for long periods with little or no free-standing water. They are usually found in small herds, although larger groups may form where food is abundant. Both males and females possess long, straight horns that are used for defence against predators and for competition within the species.

## Diet

Gemsboks are herbivores that feed primarily on grasses, leaves, shrubs, roots, bulbs, and wild fruits. During prolonged dry periods, they obtain much of their moisture from succulent plants and melons, allowing them to survive in environments where surface water is scarce.

## Ecological Importance

As large grazing and browsing herbivores, gemsboks help maintain grassland and shrubland ecosystems by influencing vegetation growth and serving as prey for large predators such as lions, leopards, and spotted hyenas.

## Major Threats

- Habitat fragmentation
- Overhunting in some regions
- Competition with livestock for grazing resources
- Disease transmission from domestic animals

## Interesting Fact

The gemsbok can tolerate extremely high body temperatures without suffering heat stress, reducing the need for evaporative cooling and helping it conserve water in desert environments.
