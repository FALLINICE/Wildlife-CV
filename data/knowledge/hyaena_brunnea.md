# Hyaena brunnea

**Common Name:** Brown Hyena

**Scientific Name:** *Hyaena brunnea*

## Conservation Status

**Near Threatened (NT)** according to the International Union for Conservation of Nature (IUCN) Red List. Brown hyena populations are relatively small and fragmented, with threats including habitat loss, persecution by humans, and accidental deaths caused by snares and poisoning.

## Habitat

Brown hyenas inhabit arid and semi-arid environments, including deserts, scrublands, savannas, and dry grasslands. They are primarily found in southern Africa, with strong populations in Namibia, Botswana, western Zimbabwe, and South Africa, particularly within the Kalahari and Namib regions.

## Behaviour

Brown hyenas are primarily nocturnal and are generally solitary while foraging, although they live in small social groups known as clans. They maintain territories using scent markings and vocalizations. Unlike the more social spotted hyena, brown hyenas rely heavily on scavenging but are also capable hunters of small animals.

## Diet

Brown hyenas are omnivorous scavengers. Their diet consists mainly of carrion, but they also eat small mammals, birds, reptiles, insects, eggs, fruits, and occasionally hunt small prey. They play an important role in removing animal carcasses from the environment.

## Ecological Importance

As efficient scavengers, brown hyenas help recycle nutrients and reduce the spread of disease by consuming carcasses that might otherwise remain in the ecosystem. They are an important component of healthy arid and savanna ecosystems.

## Major Threats

- Habitat loss and fragmentation
- Persecution by livestock farmers
- Accidental capture in snares and traps
- Poisoning associated with predator control

## Interesting Fact

Brown hyenas are easily recognized by their long, shaggy dark-brown coat and powerful jaws. Individuals can travel more than 30 kilometres (19 miles) in a single night while searching for food across their large home ranges.
